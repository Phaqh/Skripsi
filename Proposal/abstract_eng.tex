\abstracteng
Opponent modeling in repeated games is commonly formulated as a one-step prediction problem, where the next action of the opponent is predicted based on the history of interactions. However, strategic interactions in repeated games unfold sequentially over a long horizon and inherently form a closed-loop system, in which the agent's actions influence the opponent's subsequent responses. This characteristic makes multi-step prediction non-trivial, as the distribution of future opponent actions depends not only on past interactions but also on the agent's future actions.

This research investigates the role of prediction horizon in opponent modeling within the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma environment. The proposed model performs multi-step opponent action prediction (\textit{h-step forecasting}) based on observed interaction histories. The model is trained by minimizing the prediction error of the opponent's action distribution over multiple future steps.

The analysis focuses on the model's ability to perform multi-step prediction under closed-loop interactions and examines how the prediction horizon affects the representation of the opponent's strategic patterns along the interaction trajectory. The results are expected to show that extending the prediction horizon provides a richer representation of opponent strategies that depend on sequential interactions. This study highlights the importance of aligning the prediction objective in opponent modeling with the planning horizon required for decision-making in repeated games.\\

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} opponent modeling, repeated games, multi-step prediction, closed-loop interaction, Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma.
